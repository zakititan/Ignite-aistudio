# LaunchPad Pro

Build a polished, production-quality responsive web application named:

“Launch My Business Online”
Tagline: “Your simple, step-by-step guide from business idea to live website.”

PRIMARY PURPOSE
Create an interactive digital coach for non-technical small-business owners who want to set up:
1. Their own domain name
2. A professional website
3. Business email
4. Basic online presence and discoverability
5. A safe, maintainable ownership structure

The product should make an intimidating technical process feel achievable. It must avoid jargon unless it is explained in simple language. The core user promise is:

“Tell us about your business and goals. We’ll create a clear, personalized plan to get your domain and website live—without needing to understand hosting, DNS, or SEO beforehand.”

TARGET USERS
Design for:
- Local service businesses: plumbers, electricians, salons, consultants, tutors, repair shops, clinics, agencies
- Restaurants, cafés, bakeries, home chefs
- Freelancers and independent professionals
- Retailers and small ecommerce businesses
- Coaches, creators, and course sellers
- Small organizations or charities
- First-time founders with little or no technical knowledge

Assume many users:
- Have never purchased a domain
- Do not know the difference between a domain, web hosting, DNS, business email, and a website builder
- Are anxious about spending money incorrectly
- Need mobile-first guidance
- Need a website live quickly, often within one to seven days
- Want to avoid being locked out of their own website or domain account

PRODUCT PRINCIPLES
- Friendly, calm, practical, confidence-building
- No unexplained technical terminology
- Use progressive disclosure: show simple guidance first and advanced details only if requested
- Never pressure users into a single provider or imply affiliation
- Clearly separate “required”, “recommended”, and “optional”
- Promote business ownership and account security
- Make progress visually obvious
- Make every major setup task actionable with checklists, how-to explanations, links placeholders, and completion tracking
- Provide reminders, warnings, and plain-English explanations before a potentially risky step
- Make the application useful even if users do not sign in

BRAND AND VISUAL DIRECTION
Create a trustworthy, modern, approachable SaaS interface.

Visual style:
- Clean white and soft warm-gray background
- Primary color: deep indigo or royal blue for trust
- Secondary accent: teal / emerald for completed tasks and success states
- Warm amber for warnings and “important” guidance
- Coral/red only for high-risk or urgent errors
- Rounded cards, gentle shadows, 12–16px corner radius
- Spacious layout, easy scanning, large readable type
- Use friendly line icons for domain, website, email, checklist, shield/security, analytics, and launch
- Avoid overly corporate visuals, dark hacker aesthetics, or complex dashboards
- Use subtle illustrations / abstract decorative SVG elements where helpful
- Must be fully responsive with excellent mobile behavior

TYPOGRAPHY
- Use a clean modern sans-serif, such as Inter, Manrope, or similar
- Large, confident page headings
- Comfortable body copy with strong line height
- Minimum accessible text sizing
- Clear visual hierarchy

ACCESSIBILITY
- Use semantic HTML and accessible labels
- High text contrast
- Keyboard navigable controls
- Visible focus states
- Buttons must have descriptive text
- Avoid relying only on color to communicate status
- Include alt text placeholders for visual assets
- Ensure forms show clear inline validation and helpful error messages

Do not claim live availability unless an actual domain-search API is connected.

Domain recommendation rules:
- Prefer short, easy-to-spell, easy-to-say names
- Avoid hyphens and numbers where possible
- Recommend a relevant extension, but explain that the domain name itself and business fit matter more than blindly choosing a trendy extension
- Give alternatives if the preferred name is unavailable
- Suggest using a location term only when it meaningfully helps a local business
- Avoid trademark-risk claims; show a warning that users should verify they are entitled to use their business name

Create a “Domain Name Score” card evaluating:
- Clarity
- Memorability
- Ease of spelling
- Local relevance
- Brand flexibility
- Potential confusion

Add tooltips that explain each score in non-technical language.

Domain basics explainer:
Use visual cards for:
- Domain: your web address, like yourbusiness.com
- Website: the pages visitors see
- Hosting: the service that stores and delivers your website
- DNS: the settings that direct your domain to the right services
- Registrar: the company where you register and renew the domain
- SSL / HTTPS: the security that protects the connection to your site

Include a visual analogy:
“Your domain is your street address. Hosting is the building. DNS is the directory that tells visitors where to go.”

Domain safety checklist:
- Register the domain in an account you personally or jointly control
- Use a business-controlled email address
- Turn on two-factor authentication
- Add a backup recovery method
- Turn on renewal reminders or auto-renewal
- Record the registrar, renewal date, login owner, and recovery email
- Keep a second trusted business owner or administrator with documented access where appropriate
- Never share passwords through unsecured messages
- Review who has access before engaging a contractor

Show generated guided instructions using generic DNS record examples:
- A record
- CNAME record
- TXT record
- MX record
- Redirect / forwarding where appropriate

Use an editable table:
Record type | Host/Name | Value/Target | Purpose | Status | Copy action

For each record, include:
- Plain-English purpose
- Copy button
- “I added this” checkbox
- “Where do I add this?” help accordion
- “What could go wrong?” troubleshooting section

Very important safeguards:
- Display an amber warning before DNS changes:
“DNS changes can affect your website and business email. If your domain already receives email, do not remove MX records unless your email provider specifically tells you to.”
- Explain that DNS updates may take time to become visible
- Include a “Before you change anything” checklist:
  - Screenshot existing DNS records
  - Identify current website and email services
  - Avoid deleting unknown records
  - Confirm you have the correct target values from your provider
  - Make one change at a time when possible
  - Save a record of the change

Email preservation alert:
If the user indicates they have business email, show:
“Protect your email: website DNS changes and email DNS settings are related but not interchangeable. Keep your mail-related records unless you are intentionally changing email providers.”

Provide a DNS troubleshooting flow:
- My website is not loading
- I see a parking page
- My old website still appears
- My business email stopped working
- HTTPS is not active
- Verification is failing
- I am unsure which record to change

Each troubleshooting flow should use simple questions and recommendations, not unsupported technical guarantees.
