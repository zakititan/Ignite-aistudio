# Launch Plan Buddy

> A calm, step-by-step guide that helps small-business owners go from "I need a website" to "My business is live online."

Launch Plan Buddy is a guided web application for non-technical small-business owners who need to set up a domain, build a professional website, configure business email, and establish the fundamentals for a safe and discoverable online presence.

Rather than overwhelming users with technical jargon or pushing a single provider, the app turns website setup into a personalized roadmap: one clear task at a time.

## The problem

For a first-time business owner, launching a website can mean navigating unfamiliar terms, conflicting provider advice, hidden costs, account-access risks, and dozens of setup decisions.

Common questions include:

- What is the difference between a domain, hosting, a website builder, and DNS?
- Which type of website setup is right for my business?
- How do I connect my domain without breaking business email?
- What pages do customers expect to see?
- How do I make sure my website works on phones and can be found online?
- How do I ensure my business—not an agency or former employee—controls critical accounts?

Launch Plan Buddy makes these decisions approachable by explaining them in plain English and organizing the work into an actionable launch plan.

## Who it is for

Launch Plan Buddy is designed for small-business owners with limited technical experience, including:

- Local service businesses, such as salons, tradespeople, consultants, tutors, repair shops, and clinics
- Restaurants, cafés, bakeries, and home-food businesses
- Freelancers, independent professionals, coaches, and creators
- Retailers and small ecommerce businesses
- Charities, community groups, and early-stage founders

The app is particularly useful for people who have a business name or social-media presence but no domain or website; own a domain but do not know what to do next; are replacing an old website; or need to regain clarity over accounts managed by someone else.

## Core value

### Personalized launch roadmap

Users answer a short onboarding flow about their business type, goals, current setup, required website features, budget, timeline, and technical comfort. The app then creates a tailored roadmap across these stages:

1. Plan the online presence
2. Secure the domain
3. Choose a website approach
4. Create core website pages
5. Connect domain, website, and email safely
6. Test and launch
7. Improve discoverability and maintain the site

Each stage contains practical tasks, plain-language explanations, estimated effort, and progress tracking.

### Domain and digital ownership guidance

The app helps owners understand and protect the assets their business depends on:

- Domain name and registrar account
- Website platform or hosting account
- DNS provider
- Business email provider
- Analytics and search-monitoring accounts
- Payment, booking, and social-media accounts

The product emphasizes owner-controlled accounts, recovery details, two-factor authentication, renewal reminders, and documented access. It also provides a Digital Ownership Record template for recording who owns and can recover critical business systems.

### Domain name helper

The domain helper suggests business-appropriate name formats and evaluates them using easy-to-understand criteria:

- Clarity
- Memorability
- Ease of spelling
- Local relevance
- Brand flexibility
- Potential confusion

It does not claim a domain is available unless a live domain lookup integration is enabled. Instead, it offers name ideas and directs users to verify availability with a registrar.

### Website platform matcher

An interactive decision tool helps users choose a category of website setup based on their needs, rather than forcing them toward one vendor. Recommendation categories include:

| Setup category | Best suited to |
| --- | --- |
| Easy all-in-one website builder | Owners who want a straightforward, low-maintenance site quickly |
| Ecommerce-first platform | Businesses selling products online |
| Flexible CMS with managed hosting | Businesses that need greater flexibility and room to grow |
| Professional designer/developer build | Businesses with complex needs or a larger implementation budget |
| Simple one-page launch site | Owners who need an immediate, focused online presence |

The matcher explains the benefits, trade-offs, maintenance expectations, feature fit, and questions owners should ask before purchasing.

### DNS connection center

DNS is often the most intimidating part of website setup. The connection center converts it into a guided workflow, covering records such as A, CNAME, TXT, and MX records in practical language.

It includes:

- A pre-change checklist
- Editable DNS record tables with copy actions
- Clear explanations of what each record does
- A completion state for each record
- Provider-agnostic troubleshooting flows
- Warnings to preserve existing email-related records

> **Important:** DNS changes can affect a website and business email. The app warns users not to delete unknown records and not to remove mail-related records unless they are deliberately changing email providers.

### Website content builder

The content workspace helps owners prepare the core information a credible business website needs. It supports guided drafts for:

- Home
- About
- Services
- Products
- Contact
- Booking
- Menu
- Portfolio
- Testimonials
- FAQ
- Privacy and business-policy pages

For each page, users receive a purpose statement, recommended sections, fill-in-the-blank prompts, quality checks, and editable AI-assisted draft placeholders.

### Launch checklist

The central launch checklist helps users work through required, recommended, and optional tasks without losing track of progress. It covers:

- Account ownership and recovery
- Website content and contact details
- Mobile usability, links, forms, and accessibility basics
- Domain connection, redirects, HTTPS, and email preservation
- Search and local business visibility
- Analytics and conversion tracking
- Post-launch review and maintenance

Users can filter tasks, add notes, assign tasks to a teammate or contractor, and track their overall launch readiness.

### Business email guide

A guided email section helps owners set up professional addresses such as `hello@yourbusiness.com`, `support@yourbusiness.com`, or `bookings@yourbusiness.com`. It includes mailbox planning, mobile setup, signatures, recovery settings, and introductory guidance for SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.

### Search and local presence basics

The discoverability guide focuses on durable, user-friendly fundamentals rather than ranking promises:

- Clear page titles, headings, and navigation
- Original, useful descriptions of services and products
- Mobile-friendly, secure pages
- Consistent business name, address, phone number, hours, and service-area information
- Search-monitoring setup and site verification
- Sitemap and indexing guidance where supported
- Practical content ideas tailored to the business type

### Maintenance center

Launching is the beginning, not the end. The maintenance center provides recurring reminders for reviewing customer enquiries, testing forms, updating offers and hours, reviewing analytics, checking access permissions, refreshing content, and monitoring domain renewal.

## Product principles

- **Plain English first:** technical terms are introduced only when needed and explained immediately.
- **Progress over perfection:** users are encouraged to complete one useful task at a time.
- **Ownership and safety:** essential business accounts should remain under the business owner’s control.
- **Provider neutrality:** recommendations are based on needs and trade-offs, not a forced vendor choice.
- **No false guarantees:** the app does not promise search rankings, uptime, legal compliance, or domain availability.
- **Progressive disclosure:** beginner guidance appears first; advanced details are available on demand.
- **Mobile-first usability:** owners should be able to complete meaningful work from a phone.

## Key screens

### Landing page

A conversion-focused introduction to the product, featuring the main promise, common beginner concerns, a three-step explanation, product capabilities, digital-ownership guidance, FAQs, and a clear call to action.

### Onboarding wizard

A short, progress-aware questionnaire that captures:

- Business name, category, description, and location
- Local, online, or hybrid business model
- Main objective: enquiries, bookings, ecommerce, credibility, portfolio, or newsletter
- Current domain and website status
- Needed features: contact form, booking, ecommerce, gallery, blog, multilingual content, and more

### Personalized dashboard

The dashboard gives users a calm command center with:

- Overall completion percentage
- Current launch stage
- Most important next action
- Roadmap timeline
- Ownership reminders
- Quick tools and recommended learning
- Recent activity and saved notes

### Learning library and glossary

A searchable library explains domains, hosting, DNS, email, ecommerce, content, search visibility, security, maintenance, and hiring a professional. Contextual tooltips and a dedicated glossary define terms such as registrar, nameserver, A record, CNAME, MX record, TXT record, HTTPS, sitemap, indexing, SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.

## Example user scenario

**Harbor & Hearth Bakery** is a local bakery that wants to display its menu, receive custom-cake orders, use WhatsApp for enquiries, and look credible in local search.

A personalized plan could recommend:

1. Select and register a simple, owner-controlled domain.
2. Choose an easy all-in-one website approach that supports a menu, photo gallery, enquiry forms, and WhatsApp contact.
3. Prepare Home, Menu, About, Custom Cake Orders, and Contact pages.
4. Add real product images, business hours, location, and clear ordering instructions.
5. Set up a professional business email address.
6. Connect the domain while preserving existing email settings.
7. Test the website and contact path on mobile and desktop.
8. Set up search monitoring and local-profile basics.
9. Schedule a monthly content and access review.

## Technical direction

Recommended implementation stack:

- **Frontend:** React and TypeScript
- **Styling:** Tailwind CSS
- **UI components:** shadcn/ui
- **Icons:** Lucide
- **Backend / authentication / persistence:** Supabase-compatible architecture
- **Guest experience:** Local-storage persistence for in-progress plans
- **Future integrations:** domain lookup, AI content generation, analytics, calendar reminders, search-monitoring tools, and PDF export

Suggested data entities:

```text
profiles
businesses
business_requirements
launch_plans
launch_tasks
domain_profiles
website_content_drafts
maintenance_tasks
learning_progress
```

Security requirements:

- Do not store passwords, account-recovery codes, DNS credentials, or payment-card information.
- Design persistence and permissions with row-level security readiness.
- Keep API secrets server-side.
- Provide guest mode, account creation, saved plans, data deletion, and privacy controls.

## UX and accessibility

The interface should be friendly, uncluttered, responsive, and usable by beginners.

- Use semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, accessible labels, and sufficient color contrast.
- Do not rely on color alone for status.
- Use clear validation and helpful error messages.
- Show loading states and reassuring empty states.
- Keep actions descriptive, such as “Create my launch plan,” “Mark task complete,” and “Copy DNS value.”
- Use progressive disclosure to avoid exposing complex DNS and security content too early.

## AI-assisted capabilities

AI features should assist—not replace—the owner’s judgment. Potential tools include:

- Personalized roadmap generation
- Domain-name idea generation
- Homepage headline and service-description drafts
- FAQ and local-content ideas
- Simplified explanations of technical terms
- Next-priority task recommendations
- Launch-plan gap analysis
- A brief generator for hiring a web designer or agency

Every generated result should include edit, regenerate, and copy controls, along with a reminder to review generated content for accuracy before publishing.

## Design direction

The visual experience should feel capable, calm, and approachable:

- Clean white and warm-gray surfaces
- Deep indigo or blue as the primary trust color
- Teal or emerald for successful completion states
- Amber for important warnings and DNS/security guidance
- Rounded cards, subtle shadows, generous spacing, and readable typography
- A simple visual journey: `Business Idea → Domain → Website → Launch → Grow`

Avoid aggressive sales language, dark technical aesthetics, provider lock-in, and cluttered dashboard patterns.

## Safety and limitations

Launch Plan Buddy provides general educational guidance. Provider pricing, plan features, technical requirements, domain availability, legal obligations, accessibility requirements, tax rules, privacy requirements, and search visibility vary by location, business type, and platform.

Users should verify current provider documentation before making purchases or DNS changes and seek qualified professional advice when their situation requires it.
